Before · During · After · Always

The whole loop — not just the tape

Before: Walk in rehearsed

Open the lead. See what’s already known, the open gaps, and the next three moves. Play a personalized mental-rehearsal audio — breath work plus a visualization written for this rep, this lead, this appointment.

Pull up calm, focused, and already having run the close once in your head — so that paid lead isn’t burned on winging it.

During: Sell — the app takes the notes

One-tap appointment recording. The rep enrolls their voice once so the app can tell which words were theirs and which were the customer’s. Or run live voice practice between stops in the truck — spoken out loud, hands on the wheel, no typing.

No script to read, no prompts on a screen. The phone stays in your pocket while you sell — the notes are waiting in the truck.

After: Coaching in minutes, not Mondays

What actually happened in the deal, what the customer revealed, what’s still unknown, next moves, and a follow-up drafted from what was said, ready to copy into your own texts — while the appointment is still fresh. Every breakdown picks up where the last one left off — it knows what you’ve been working on. No scores. No grades. No report cards. Ever.

Follow-ups that sound like you leave before you’re out of the driveway — and the next appointment starts sharper.

Always: Practice the hard ones

Role-play the exact customer you’re stuck on. Live voice practice that already knows the deal — and what you’ve been working on. Goals set by voice, in the rep’s own words.

Blow the close in practice — not in the living room. New reps practice on the AI, not on your leads.

Built different on purpose

Reps use it because it’s theirs.

Every training tool fails the same way: reps feel policed, so they stop using it. Blue Collar Close is built the other way around. The rep’s side of the app and the company’s side are separate rooms with a wall between them. Recordings, drills, and rehearsal history stay in the rep’s room — the business side sees the pipeline and deal stage, never the tapes. The training stays private. The results show up in the closes.

What makes it different

A framework, not a guess

Every appointment is broken down the same way, every time, by a deterministic analysis engine — a framework built on contemporary education science, sales method, game theory, and performance psychology. The same tape gets the same read. No AI mood swings.

It grows with the rep

The app keeps track of what each rep has been working on and carries it forward — every appointment, every lead adds to one continuous story. This month builds on last month, not fifty disconnected sessions.

Voice-first, no clutter

Built for a rep in a truck, not an analyst at a desk. A deliberately minimalist interface with the details sweated — and voice at the center: talk to it like a person, set goals out loud, keep your eyes on the road.

Questions people actually ask

Is recording appointments legal?

Recording rules vary by state (some require consent from all parties). Blue Collar Close supports disclosure practices so your team can be clear with customers. Check your state’s law and your company’s policy — this is not legal advice.

Will it actually get used?

Tools that feel like homework or policing die in a month. The training is private to the rep — the business side sees pipeline and stage, not tapes. Reps stick with what makes them money and doesn’t write them up after a long day.

Is this another CRM to migrate to?

No. It’s a performance training app that rides alongside how you already run leads. No CRM integrations promised on day one — and no rip-and-replace pitch.

What does it cost?

We’re in early access. Plans will include Solo and Team/Org — we’re not publishing public pricing yet. Talk to us; we onboard a few at a time.

Do managers hear the calls?

No. The app is built as two separate spaces. Managers see the pipeline and deal stage only; transcripts, training content, and rep development stay in the rep’s space. See the privacy block.
